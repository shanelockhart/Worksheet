# Fitment glossary and formulas (general guidance)

- Lug / bolt pattern (PCD): number of studs x diameter of the circle they sit on, in mm (5x114.3 = 5 studs on a 114.3 mm circle). 5x114.3 equals 5x4.5 in. Patterns that look close (5x114.3 vs 5x115, 5x120 vs 5x120.65) are NOT interchangeable.
- Center bore (CB): the hole in the wheel's back that centers it on the hub. Vehicle hub bore must be less than or equal to wheel CB for a wheel to fit. If the wheel CB is larger, hub-centric rings fill the gap. A wheel CB smaller than the hub does not fit.
- Thread size / pitch: stud or bolt thread such as M12x1.5, M14x1.5, M14x1.25. Must match the vehicle exactly.
- Seat type: how the lug nut contacts the wheel: conical (tapered, most aftermarket and many Japanese vehicles), ball/radius (many European vehicles, e.g. BMW M14x1.25 uses a ball seat on factory wheels unless noted), flat/mag. Mismatched seat is unsafe.
- Offset (ET): distance in mm from the wheel's mounting face to its centerline. Higher positive offset tucks the wheel inward; lower or negative pushes it outward. Changing offset changes clearance to suspension, brakes and fenders.
- Backspacing: distance from the mounting face to the wheel's inner edge, in inches. Approximate: backspacing (in) = (wheel width in x 25.4 / 2 + offset mm) / 25.4.
- Wheel width and diameter: e.g. 17x8 = 17 in diameter, 8 in width. Each tire width has a recommended range of rim widths.
- Tire size: 265/70R17 = 265 mm section width, sidewall height 70 percent of width, radial, fits a 17 in wheel.
  - Sidewall height (mm) = width x aspect ratio / 100
  - Overall diameter (in) = wheel diameter + 2 x sidewall mm / 25.4
  - Percent difference vs. stock = (new diameter - old diameter) / old diameter x 100. Under about 3 percent is commonly considered acceptable; speedometer reads low when the new tire is taller. Actual speed = indicated speed x (new diameter / old diameter).
- Plus sizing: increase wheel diameter and lower the aspect ratio so overall diameter stays about the same.
- Load index and speed rating: printed after the size (e.g. 116S). The tire's load index must be at least what the vehicle requires; the door-jamb placard lists the original requirement.
- TPMS: tire pressure sensors; new wheels typically need sensors moved or replaced.
- Hub-centric vs lug-centric: hub-centric wheels are centered by the hub bore, lug-centric by the lug nuts.
